# Key Tax Figures — 2026 (MFJ)

**Summary**: Every dollar figure the rest of the [[tax-mitigation-master-plan|tax plan]] depends on, in one place, for tax year 2026 and married-filing-jointly. **Refresh this note every November** when the IRS releases the next year's inflation adjustments — the strategies are durable, these numbers are not.
**Sources**: IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32, IRS Notice 2025-67, CMS 2026 Medicare premium announcement, OBBBA (P.L. 119-21)
**Verify before acting**: anything marked ⚠️

## Federal income tax brackets — MFJ, taxable income

| Rate | Taxable income |
|---|---|
| 10% | $0 – $24,800 |
| 12% | $24,800 – $100,800 |
| 22% | $100,800 – $211,400 |
| 24% | $211,400 – **$403,550** |
| 32% | $403,550 – $512,450 |
| 35% | $512,450 – $768,700 |
| 37% | over $768,700 |

**Standard deduction (MFJ)**: $32,200

**The two numbers that matter most for conversions:**
- Top of 22% → **$243,600 AGI** ($211,400 + $32,200)
- Top of 24% → **$435,750 AGI** ($403,550 + $32,200)

## Long-term capital gains / qualified dividends — MFJ

| Rate | Taxable income |
|---|---|
| 0% | up to **$96,700** |
| 15% | $96,700 – ~$620,000 ⚠️ *confirm exact* |
| 20% | above that |

**NIIT**: additional 3.8% on net investment income once MAGI exceeds **$250,000** (MFJ). Not indexed — it never moves. Top effective LTCG rate = **23.8%**.

LTCG and qualified dividends **stack on top of** ordinary income. Ordinary income (including Roth conversions) fills the ordinary brackets first; capital gains are then taxed in their own brackets above it. So a large conversion does not get taxed at capital-gains rates, but it *does* push capital gains out of the 0% band.

## Medicare IRMAA — 2026 premiums (based on 2024 MAGI)

| 2024 MAGI (MFJ) | Part B / mo | Part D surcharge / mo | Annual cost, couple |
|---|---|---|---|
| ≤ $218,000 | $202.90 | — | $4,870 |
| $218,001 – $274,000 | $284.10 | +$14.50 | $7,166 |
| $274,001 – $342,000 | $405.80 | +$37.50 | $10,640 |
| $342,001 – **$410,000** | $527.50 | +$60.40 | $14,110 |
| $410,001 – $750,000 | $649.30 | +$83.30 ⚠️ | $17,582 |
| over $750,000 | $689.90 | +$91.00 ⚠️ | $18,742 |

IRMAA is a **cliff, not a phase-in** — one dollar over a boundary triggers the entire next tier for both spouses for the full year. The **$410,000 boundary** is the operative ceiling for our conversion program; see [[roth-conversion-window]] for why we stop there rather than at the top of the 24% bracket.

## Loss limitations and deduction caps

| Item | 2026 (MFJ) |
|---|---|
| Excess business loss — §461(l) | **$512,000** (down from $626,000 in 2025) |
| NOL carryforward usage cap | 80% of taxable income |
| Passive loss special allowance — §469(i) | $25,000, phased out $100K–$150K MAGI → **unavailable to us** |
| SALT cap | $40,400 |
| SALT phase-down begins (MAGI) | **$505,000** — cap reduced by 30% of the excess, floored at $10,000 |
| Bonus depreciation | **100%, permanent** (property acquired after 1/19/2025) |
| §179 expensing | $2.5M, phase-out begins $4M |

**The SALT torpedo**: between **$505,000 and ~$606,000 MAGI**, every extra dollar of income also destroys 30¢ of SALT deduction. In the 35% bracket that makes the true marginal rate **45.5%**. Deductions that pull MAGI down through this band are worth far more than their face rate.

## Estate

- Estate/gift exemption: **$15M per person / $30M MFJ**, permanent and indexed (OBBBA). Unless net worth exceeds $30M, estate tax is not the binding constraint — **basis step-up planning is**.
- Non-spouse heirs of traditional IRAs: **10-year distribution rule**, taxed at the heir's rates. Roth inherits the 10-year rule but distributions are tax-free — this is a large part of the case for converting.
