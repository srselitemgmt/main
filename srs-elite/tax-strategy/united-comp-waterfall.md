# United Compensation Waterfall — Where the 18% Actually Goes

**Summary**: United contributes **18% of Ryan's eligible compensation** to retirement, and it fills three buckets in a fixed order: the **PRAP/401(k)** to the §415(c) limit, then the **[[rha-retiree-health-account|RHA]]** until its cap (reached around **$420K of YTD gross**), then **PRAP Cash — taxable wages** for the rest of the year. At ~$800K gross that last bucket is roughly **$65K/year taxed at ~42%**, and it is **currently unavoidable**. The fix is already negotiated: the union and company have agreed to a **Market-Based Cash Balance Plan (MBCBP)** turning on **late 2027 or 2028**, and Ryan will elect the excess into it instead of cash. That single election is worth **~$8–9K/year in rate arbitrage alone**, plus pre-tax compounding on the whole amount — the largest remaining tax lever in the working years.
**Source**: Ryan, 2026-08-13; corroborated by the July 2026 pay advice

## The waterfall

| Order | Bucket | Fills until | Tax treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **PRAP / 401(k)** | §415(c) limit ($72,000 in 2026) | Pre-tax, tax-deferred |
| 2 | **[[rha-retiree-health-account|RHA]]** | its cap — reached at **~$420K YTD gross** | **Never taxed** (in *and* out, for qualified medical) |
| 3 | **PRAP Cash** | rest of the calendar year | **Taxable W-2 wages** — ~42% all-in |
| 4 *(from 2027–28)* | **MBCBP** | replaces bucket 3 by election | Pre-tax, tax-deferred |

Two things follow immediately:

- **Bucket 2 is why the RHA grows so fast.** The RHA isn't just the $1.00/hour employee trickle on the pay stub — it's the primary landing spot for employer contributions above the §415(c) limit. That explains a $260,893 balance and the >$350K-by-2027 trajectory in [[rha-retiree-health-account]]. It also means the "ask about a spill election" action from the earlier review was already answered: **the RHA spill is automatic and already maxed each year.**
- **Bucket 3 is unavoidable until the MBCBP exists**, which corrects the prior recommendation. There is no election available today that keeps those dollars out of taxable wages.

## The MBCBP election — the single largest remaining working-years lever

A Market-Based Cash Balance Plan is a **defined benefit** plan, so it is governed by the §415(b) *annual benefit* limit rather than the §415(c) *contribution* limit that the PRAP already exhausts. That is precisely why it can absorb the overflow: it's a different limit entirely, with a ceiling far above what this spill will ever reach.

**Value of electing it over PRAP Cash:**

| | PRAP Cash (today) | MBCBP (2028+) |
|---|---|---|
| $65,000 of employer money | taxed ~42% → **$38,000 net** | **$65,000 pre-tax, compounding** |
| Later, on conversion/withdrawal | already taxed; growth taxed annually in a taxable account | ~28% all-in on the fast conversion lane ([[roth-conversion-window]]) |
| **Rate arbitrage** | — | **~14 points ≈ $9,000/year** |

Over the ~13 years from 2028 to Ryan's 2041 retirement, that's roughly **$845K of additional pre-tax contributions** instead of ~$490K of after-tax cash — before counting the difference between tax-deferred and taxable-account compounding.

> [!warning] It does make the conversion problem bigger — and it's still clearly worth it
> MBCBP money is pre-tax and rolls to a traditional IRA at retirement, so it lands squarely on the [[roth-conversion-window|$5–6M pile]] that already strains the nine-year window. But deferring at **42% and converting at ~28%** is a 14-point win on every dollar, and the alternative isn't "less pre-tax" — it's "the same money, taxed at 42% today." **Elect it.** The conversion program absorbs it via the fast lane, cost-seg sheltering, and QCDs.

## An open question worth asking benefits

Ryan's **after-tax 401(k) contributions ($9,266 YTD)** consume §415(c) space. If employer money is allocated *after* employee contributions, then every after-tax dollar he adds pushes an employer dollar further down the waterfall — and once the RHA is capped, that means **pushing it into taxable PRAP Cash**.

If that's how the plan orders it, the trade is roughly:

| | Contribute $1 after-tax | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| In the plan | $1 → Roth (worth ~$1.00) | $1 employer pre-tax (worth ~$0.72 after future conversion) |
| In pocket | displaced employer $1 → PRAP Cash → **$0.58** | keeps the $1 of after-tax cash |
| **Total** | **~$1.58** | **~$1.72** |

— which would make the after-tax contributions **mildly value-destroying during the spill months**, and better restricted to early in the year before the RHA cap is hit.

**This is plan-specific and I can't verify the allocation order from the documents I have.** Ask benefits: *"Are employer contributions allocated before or after employee after-tax contributions for §415(c) purposes, and do my after-tax contributions increase my PRAP Cash?"* If the answer is that employer money is allocated first and is unaffected, the after-tax contributions are pure upside and should continue at maximum — the mega-backdoor logic in [[schwab-portfolio-review-2026-07]] holds unchanged.
