### How the United PRAP actually fills up — and the three actions it implies

United's **direct employer contribution** (~16% of eligible comp, capped by the $360K compensation limit ≈ **$57,600/yr**) consumes most of the $72,000 §415(c) limit before Ryan defers a dollar. That's why his pre-tax deferral is small and why excess **spills to "PRAP Cash" — taxable wages at his ~45% all-in marginal rate**. Three concrete moves:

1. **Turn on the age-50 catch-up now.** Ryan turned 50 on 8/3/2026, so he's catch-up eligible for all of 2026 — and the **$8,000 catch-up sits *outside* the §415(c) limit**, meaning it's incremental tax-advantaged space the spillover can't crowd out. It must be Roth (the mandate applies). **No catch-up line appears on the stub yet — this is being left on the table as of July.**
2. **Check the spill election.** United pilots can typically direct 415(c)/comp-limit excess to the **RHA instead of taxable PRAP Cash** ⚠️ *(verify with benefits)*. $10,601 YTD has gone out as cash taxed at ~45%; the same dollars into the RHA would be never-taxed. If the election exists, this is worth ~$4–8K/year of tax, every year to 2041.
3. **Convert the after-tax money to Roth promptly.** The $9,266 of after-tax deferrals (and the PRAP post-tax $4,500) should be swept to Roth (in-plan conversion or split rollover) before meaningful earnings accrue — earnings on after-tax money are pre-tax until converted. This may be exactly what account #6686 is; see the $70K question.

## The strategic finding: the pre-tax pile is on track to outrun the conversion window

$1.75M pre-tax at 50, compounding ~15 years with **~$60–75K/yr of employer contributions that can't be turned off**, lands around **$5–6M+ at Ryan's 2041 retirement** (7% assumption). The [[roth-conversion-window#Which lane|slow lane]] clears ~$3.7M over nine years; even the **fast lane (~$6.4M nominal capacity) barely covers it** — and with Stacy holding no retirement accounts, this balance IS the entire conversion problem.

Consequences, in order:

1. **Every future dollar that can avoid the pre-tax pile, should.** The catch-up is Roth by mandate (good — turn it on), the after-tax→Roth sweep adds Roth, Stacy's solo 401(k) leans Roth, and the RHA spill election moves employer excess to a never-taxed bucket instead of cash. None of these reduce the *employer* pre-tax flow — that's fixed — but they stop adding to the problem voluntarily.
2. **Plan on the fast lane** ($750K MAGI ceiling, ~28.4% all-in) for at least Ryan's window, and re-run the ladder each year — [[roth-conversion-window]] updated.
3. **REPS-sheltered conversions become even more valuable** — every $1 of cost-seg loss in 2042–2050 is a dollar of conversion at ~0% instead of 28% ([[roth-conversion-window#Sheltering conversions with real estate losses]]).
4. **QCDs and the RMD-era plan absorb the residual** — with QCD capacity of $222K/couple/year (2026 terms) from the 70½s, an unconverted remainder is manageable, not catastrophic. But the widow's-penalty math still argues for converting hard.
