## The plan in six sentences

1. **Defer everything possible now** at a 35–45% effective rate — Ryan's dollars pre-tax, **Stacy's dollars leaning Roth** because her conversion window is only four years.
2. **Run the real estate portfolio through Stacy's REPS** — her full-time real estate work makes *every* rental's cost-seg losses (MHPs and RV parks included) deductible against Ryan's W-2 income, up to the $512K/year excess-business-loss cap.
3. **Relocate to a no-income-tax state before 2041** — federal law then bars the former state from ever taxing the retirement distributions.
4. **Convert Stacy's traditional balances hard in 2042–2045** (before her RMDs start in 2046), sheltered by cost-seg losses and capped below the $410K IRMAA cliff; **Ryan's convert 2046–2050**.
5. **Each spouse claims Social Security at 70** — Stacy 2/2041, Ryan 8/2046 — maximizing the survivor benefit.
6. **Never sell what gets a basis step-up** — convert the IRAs (no step-up), hold the brokerage and real estate (step-up erases all depreciation recapture).

## The three structural problems (from the age gap)

Detail in [[household-timeline]]:

1. **Stacy reaches Medicare in 2/2036 — 5½ years before Ryan retires** — ✅ **Decided (2026-08-13): she stays on Ryan's employer coverage until his 8/2041 retirement**, penalty-free, with the [[rha-retiree-health-account|RHA]] funding both spouses' premiums after that.
2. **2041 is a partial year** (Ryan retires 8/3/2041) — first clean conversion year is 2042.
3. **Stacy's conversion window is four years; Ryan's is nine.** Her balances convert first; her new savings go Roth now to shrink the problem.
