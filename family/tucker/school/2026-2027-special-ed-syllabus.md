## TL;DR

Attendance is the backbone of everything in this program — intervention classes build day on day and are the hardest thing to make up, and Homecoming eligibility is tied to specific attendance thresholds ("AttenDANCE HOCO"). Phones are away bell to bell, students send home a weekly grade check, and the teacher asks to be cc'd on any email to school staff about the IEP, services, or progress.

## AttenDANCE HOCO — Homecoming attendance standard

To attend Homecoming, students must stay within these limits (tracked through the fall; the teacher will reach out well before Homecoming if a student's standing is at risk):

1. No more than **10 tardies**
2. No more than **5 partial absences** (arriving more than 5 minutes late to a class)
3. No more than **3 single-period unexcused absences**

## Missing and late work in intervention classes

- Intervention classes (Literacy Labs and other specialized instruction periods) build directly on the prior day's skill, so missed sessions are genuinely hard to recreate with makeup work.
- Illness and emergencies are always excused without question.
- The ask: where possible, schedule appointments and non-essential commitments **outside of intervention time**.
- When a session is missed, the teacher will help the student catch up — but consistent attendance is the single most effective support.

## Cell phone policy

Phones are put away **bell to bell** — from the start of class to the end of class, every period.

## Weekly grade checks

Students send home a weekly grade check. The purpose is building executive functioning skills: planning ahead, tracking progress, and knowing when to ask for help.

## Support classes — what to expect

Executive functioning skills (planning, organization, task initiation, self-monitoring) are woven into every lab. Math Lab and the Literacy Labs build them alongside content instruction; **Learning Lab is where executive functioning is the explicit, primary focus**.

### Math Lab

Targeted support aligned with the student's current general education math class: preteaching upcoming concepts, reteaching, guided practice, and time for current assignments with support close at hand.

Students are asked to:
- Bring current math materials and any assignments in progress
- Engage in preteaching/reteaching even when content feels familiar
- Use guided practice to build accuracy and confidence, not just finish work
- Speak up when a concept isn't making sense rather than moving on unsure

### Learning Lab

Direct instruction in executive functioning — planning time, organizing materials, starting tasks, monitoring progress — plus time to stay current on assignments across other classes.

Students are asked to:
- Bring materials and a running list of assignments from all classes
- Participate in the organizational systems and planning routines practiced together
- Use the time to build habits (breaking down tasks, planning backward from due dates), not only to finish work
- Track progress with whatever tool works best — planner, checklist, or digital system

### Social Communications Learning Lab

Designed for students identified with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) who benefit from direct support building social communication skills: reading social cues, perspective-taking, and navigating peer interactions in a structured setting. Staffed collaboratively by two special education teachers, the speech-language pathologist, the school psychologist/social worker, and paraeducators.

Students are asked to:
- Participate actively in structured social skill practice, even outside their comfort zone
- Practice new skills in real interactions, not just discussion
- Stay open to team feedback as a normal part of learning
- Carry skills from the lab into other classes and settings

### LANGUAGE!Live Literacy Lab (Years 1, 2, and 3)

A structured literacy program blending foundational reading instruction (phonics, word study, fluency) with grade-level vocabulary, comprehension, and writing. Mix of whole-group instruction, guided practice, and adaptive computer-based lessons. Year 1 builds foundational skills; Year 3 continues with more advanced text and writing work.

Students are asked to:
- Come ready to participate in both whole-group and independent work time
- Bring the LANGUAGE!Live workbook and **headphones** each day
- Give honest effort on the adaptive computer lessons
- Complete assigned practice tied to the week's skill focus
- Ask for help when something is unclear rather than skipping past it
